# AI at the Frontier of Discovery: Opportunities and Challenges in the LLM Era

**Audience:** Dalian University of Technology alumni (大工校友). **Format:** 30 minutes including brief audience participation and a panel handoff. **Language:** English with Chinese key messages. **Evidence review date:** October 2, 2026, America/Los_Angeles. Event date and speaker biography have not been supplied.

## Recommended abstract

Large language models are becoming components of systems that reason, use tools and assist research. Recent mathematical work—including OpenAI’s September 2026 announcement of a proposed Navier–Stokes solution—offers a timely example of both the promise of AI-assisted discovery and the need to examine exact claims, verification artifacts and independent acceptance. Scientific AI also extends beyond LLMs: specialized models, research agents and experimental tools contribute to molecular science, drug discovery and engineering. Drawing on these examples and the repository’s Wave Intelligence perspective, this talk explores a progression from predicting properties and generating candidates toward testing explanations of how systems behave. It connects that frontier to the careers of Dalian University of Technology alumni: choosing valuable tasks, learning complementary skills, measuring complete workflows and retaining human responsibility. The central message is practical: use AI to expand exploration, use evidence to determine action and build professional value through judgment, verification and reliable execution.

The dated mathematical reference is attributed to [OpenAI’s announcement](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/); discussion of independent acceptance is grounded in [Clay’s prize rules](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/rules/).
