# Panel questions and concise answer cards

Use these as preparation notes. Attribute specific claims to the cited research; career answers are recommendations rather than forecasts.

## Did AI solve Navier–Stokes?

Suggested answer: “OpenAI published a proposed solution and formal artifacts in September 2026. I am preserving the theorem’s exact scope and distinguishing that publication from independent acceptance. This talk does not certify the proof.” If pressed, direct the discussion to [the manuscript](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/32d9f210-8b73-45e0-91bc-82a30aef8a9a/navier-stokes.pdf), [Lean repository](https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler) and [Clay’s official formulation](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf). Avoid turning a panel answer into an improvised proof review.

## Does Lean checking remove the need for mathematicians?

Suggested answer: “A checker validates a formally expressed statement under its rules and assumptions. Experts still need to assess the statement, definitions, assumptions and relationship to the intended problem. Mathematical insight and interpretation remain separate questions.” This package did not run the published Lean build.

## If a singularity exists, are engineering simulations unreliable?

Suggested answer: “A global mathematical claim under specified data does not automatically invalidate a simulation in a validated operating regime. Engineering models need suitable assumptions, numerical checks and comparison with relevant physical measurements.” Keep the distinction between theorem scope and application domain visible.

## Is AlphaFold an LLM?

Suggested answer: “AlphaFold is a specialized scientific modeling family. It belongs to the wider AI-for-science ecosystem. The talk discusses how language interfaces, specialized models and experiments can work together.” Cite [AlphaFold 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07487-w) for its studied prediction task.

## Have AI-designed drugs already proved that AI can cure disease?

Suggested answer: “AI-supported discovery has produced candidates that reached clinical studies. A trial stage is not equivalent to broad efficacy or approval. Rentosertib’s published phase 2a study is an example of a safety-focused early clinical test.” Refer to [the trial paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03743-2). Do not provide treatment advice or infer current approval from the paper.

## Does Wave Intelligence already establish mechanisms?

Suggested answer: “The repository paper proposes a framework and evaluation agenda. Its central idea is to test explanations against changing conditions while preserving alternatives. The talk’s animation is a synthetic illustration, not validation of the framework.” Attribute [Shengchao Liu’s perspective](../wave/Wave_Intelligence.pdf).

## Will AI replace engineers or researchers?

Suggested answer: “The evidence here does not establish a universal employment forecast. Tasks and workflows can change substantially. A useful response is to develop domain judgment, evaluation skills and the ability to integrate tools into accountable work.” Avoid promises that every job is safe or predictions that every profession will disappear.

## Should young alumni still learn programming and mathematics?

Suggested answer: “Yes. Foundational knowledge makes it possible to recognize errors, choose a meaningful test and understand the result. Use assistance to practice and obtain feedback, then test your understanding on a fresh problem without help.” This is a learning recommendation.

## Why do studies sometimes find gains and sometimes slowdowns?

Suggested answer: “They study different tools, populations, tasks and metrics. Review and rework can outweigh drafting gains. Historical results should retain their dates, and newer measurements can have their own biases. Measure the complete workflow you plan to use.” Sources: [METR 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), [METR 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) and [support-study v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v2).

## Which tool should I buy?

Suggested answer: “Start by specifying the task and evaluator, then compare suitable tools using approved inputs and measured outcomes. Capability, integration, privacy, cost and reliability all matter.” This package intentionally contains no current price list or universal vendor ranking.

## What can a small organization do without training its own model?

Suggested answer: “Choose a bounded workflow using existing tools, prepare approved sources, write acceptance checks and name a reviewer. The opportunity can be in better integration and evaluation rather than new model training.” Use the [30-day playbook](04-alumni-playbook.md).

## What is the biggest risk?

Suggested answer: “It depends on the workflow. An unsupported statement, data exposure or an incorrect action can be more serious in one context than another. Match permissions and review to consequences instead of assuming one risk dominates every task.” Reference [NIST](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) as voluntary guidance.

## Can we trust a model that sounds certain?

Suggested answer: “Use the evidence and evaluator. Confidence expressed in language does not necessarily represent a calibrated probability. Ask what was checked and what remains uncertain.”

## Three questions to invite from the panel

1. Which alumni problem has both meaningful value and a credible evaluator?
2. Which domain skill has become more important as AI generates more candidate answers?
3. What would convince your organization to expand, revise or stop an AI pilot?

## A useful response to an unknown fact

“I do not have verified evidence for that detail. I can distinguish the claim we checked from the question that remains open.” Add the question to the evidence-review queue; do not invent a date, capability, medical status or percentage.
