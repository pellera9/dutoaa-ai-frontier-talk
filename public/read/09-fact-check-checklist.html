Pre-event fact-check checklist

Last source review: October 2, 2026. Status: prepared research package; no claim of independent proof certification or universal correctness.

One week before the event

  1. Reopen the Navier–Stokes announcement, manuscript, formal repository and official Clay formulation. Look for corrections and changes to the precise claim.
  2. Check whether independent mathematical reviews, reproductions or formal-audit reports have appeared. Do not infer general acceptance from a company announcement or a single favorable commentary.
  3. Inspect Clay’s page and prize rules. Treat page labels as administrative observations rather than a complete consensus survey.
  4. Recheck the clinical paper and any authoritative updates before making a current trial or approval claim. This package uses only the dated phase 2a example.
  5. Confirm that productivity results still retain their original dates, populations and metrics. The support study is pinned to its November 2024 version.
  6. Preserve Wave Intelligence’s status as a conceptual perspective unless new evidence supports a different description.
  7. Review every added numerical claim against a primary source, including units, denominator, baseline and uncertainty.
  8. Record the date, scope of review and any claims you could not establish.

Immediately before speaking

  • Keep evidence labels visible on the relevant slides.
  • Say “reported,” “proposed” or “author-stated” when the evidence warrants that attribution.
  • Keep the forced formulation attached to the mathematical example.
  • Do not imply that a structure prediction establishes function or clinical benefit.
  • Do not describe historical workplace findings as current universal effects.
  • Label career examples as suggestions, rather than existing DUT programs.
  • Keep synthetic demos separate from empirical evidence.
  • Read the talk aloud and keep the qualifiers when trimming.

Automation boundary

The repository’s link checker records whether a URL can be fetched. A successful HTTP request establishes accessibility. It does not establish truth, scholarly acceptance, replication, regulatory status or fitness for a consequential decision. A failed request can reflect rate limiting or access controls rather than an incorrect citation. Review both kinds of result manually.

No automated feed is presented as verified frontier news. The interactive evidence explorer searches the curated source snapshot shipped with the package; it is dynamic in the browser, while its factual content remains reviewable and versioned.
